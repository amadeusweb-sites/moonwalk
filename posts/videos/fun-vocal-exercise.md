# Fun Vocal Exercises

## Video Script

Ready to wake up your voice? These quick, playful exercises can help you warm up for speaking, singing, or simply having fun. Keep everything easy and comfortable—no straining.

**[Exercise 1: Easy breathing]**  
Sit or stand tall, relax your shoulders, and breathe in gently. Breathe out on a soft “sss” sound, as if letting air out of a balloon. Try it once more, keeping the sound steady rather than forcing it.

**[Exercise 2: Gentle humming]**  
Close your lips comfortably and hum “mmm” at an easy pitch. Notice the gentle vibration around your lips. Keep it relaxed, then take a breath and try again.

**[Exercise 3: Lip bubbles]**  
Let your lips flutter loosely while you breathe out, like a tiny motorboat. If it feels comfortable, glide your voice gently from low to high and back down. If your lips don’t bubble, just hum instead.

**[Exercise 4: Siren sounds]**  
Make a soft “woo” sound and glide between a comfortable low and high note, like a siren passing in the distance. Keep the range small and easy.

**[Exercise 5: Tongue twister]**  
Let’s try: “Red lorry, yellow lorry.” Start slowly and clearly. Then repeat it a little faster, while keeping the words understandable. Speed is optional—clarity is the goal.

That’s your mini voice warm-up. Stay hydrated, keep your voice relaxed, and stop if anything feels uncomfortable. Which exercise was your favorite?

---

## Key Points

- Start with relaxed breathing and posture.
- Use gentle sounds in a comfortable pitch and volume.
- Try humming, lip bubbles, easy pitch glides, and a tongue twister.
- Focus on clarity and ease, not loudness or range.
- Stop if you feel pain, strain, or discomfort.

---

## Remember These

- A vocal warm-up should feel comfortable, not forced.
- Your natural speaking voice is enough; there’s no need to reach extreme notes.
- Take a breath whenever you need one.
- Lip bubbles can be replaced with a gentle hum.
- A little practice is more useful than pushing your voice.

---

## Tips

- Stand or sit comfortably with your shoulders relaxed.
- Drink water and give your voice a gentle start.
- Keep each exercise short and repeat only if it still feels easy.
- Slow down tongue twisters to make every word clear.
- Use these exercises before a presentation, rehearsal, or conversation.

---

## Takeaways

- Warm up gently before asking more of your voice.
- Keep your breath steady and your throat relaxed.
- Choose a comfortable pitch and volume.
- Stop if an exercise causes discomfort.

---

## FAQs

- **How long should I do these exercises?**  
  A few comfortable minutes is enough for a quick warm-up. Take breaks as needed.
- **What if I can’t do a lip bubble?**  
  Try a gentle “mmm” hum instead; the exercise should not feel like a struggle.
- **Should I practice at a high volume?**  
  No. Use an easy, conversational volume and avoid forcing your voice.
- **What if my throat feels sore or strained?**  
  Stop the exercises and rest your voice. Don’t push through pain or discomfort.

## Exercises

- **Steady “sss”:** Breathe out on a soft “sss” for a comfortable length of time. Repeat once without forcing the breath.
- **Hum and glide:** Hum “mmm,” then gently move your pitch up and down within an easy range.
- **Motorboat lips:** Try a relaxed lip bubble. If it doesn’t happen easily, switch to humming.
- **Clear tongue twister:** Say “Red lorry, yellow lorry” slowly three times, aiming for clear sounds rather than speed.
