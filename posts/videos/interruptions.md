# Handling Interruptions While Public Speaking

Have you ever been interrupted in the middle of a presentation? It can throw you off—but you can handle it calmly and keep your message on track.

First, pause. Take a breath, look toward the person, and avoid speaking over them. A brief pause gives you a moment to think and shows the audience you’re in control.

Next, listen long enough to understand what they need. If it’s a quick, relevant question, answer it briefly and connect your response back to your point.

If the interruption is off-topic or needs a longer discussion, acknowledge it and set a boundary. You could say, “That’s a useful question. To stay on time, let’s discuss it after the presentation.”

If someone keeps interrupting, stay respectful and be clear: “I’d like to hear your thoughts. Let me finish this point, and then I’ll come back to you.”

Then return to your presentation with a simple signpost: “As I was saying, the next key point is…” You don’t need to apologize for continuing.

Interruptions happen. Pause, respond with purpose, and guide the room back to your message. With practice, you can stay composed and make space for questions without losing your flow.

---

## Key Points

- Pause and breathe before responding.
- Listen to understand whether the interruption is relevant or urgent.
- Keep answers short and connected to your message.
- Set a polite boundary when a discussion needs more time.
- Use a clear transition to return to your presentation.

---

## Remember These

- You can be courteous and still protect your speaking time.
- A pause feels longer to you than it does to the audience.
- Not every interruption requires an immediate, full answer.
- Your goal is to guide the conversation, not win an argument.

---

## Tips

- Prepare a few phrases for redirecting questions.
- Keep your place with a note, slide heading, or key phrase.
- Make eye contact and use a steady, neutral tone.
- If the interruption is urgent, address it before continuing.
- Offer to follow up afterward when the topic deserves more time.

---

## Takeaways

- Stay calm: pause before you speak.
- Respond briefly and respectfully.
- Redirect off-topic or repeated interruptions.
- Resume with a signpost so the audience can follow you.

---

## FAQs

- **Should I answer every question immediately?**  
  No. Answer brief, relevant questions when appropriate; offer to follow up on questions that need more time.
- **What if someone interrupts repeatedly?**  
  Acknowledge them, ask to finish your point, and offer a specific time to hear them.
- **What if I lose my place?**  
  Pause, check your notes or slide, and restate the last key idea before continuing.
- **How do I handle a rude interruption?**  
  Keep your voice even, address the comment rather than the person, and return to your main point.

---

## Exercises

- **Practice the pause:** Have a partner interrupt you during a one-minute talk. Pause, take one breath, and continue.
- **Try a redirect:** Practice saying, “That’s a good question. Let’s return to it after I finish this point.”
- **Find your place:** Deliver a short talk using three key phrases in your notes. Ask a partner to interrupt, then use a phrase to resume.
